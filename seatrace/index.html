01 · Business goal & problem

Farm to fork, with the trace broken in the middle.

Avanti Frozen Foods, one of India's largest shrimp exporters, ran on a legacy ERP with no batch-level traceability. When something goes wrong downstream, the system cannot answer: which farm, which pond, which day, which lots did it touch.

  • Recalls widen because the source batch cannot be isolated.
  • Global buyers demand compliance proof the system cannot produce.
  • Planning runs blind: which purchase orders are at risk, which buyers matter this week.
  • Who set the goal: PatternLab's founders, with the exporter as joint-venture partner, committed to a traceability-first platform. Strategy was set above me; I owned making it real.
  • How it reached me: as a vision and a partner commitment, not a backlog. My translation: founders' vision + SME and demo feedback + market and competitor analysis → a 5-epic MVP roadmap. The contract I held: traceability core first, everything else earns its place.

02 · Team structure & collaboration

A 14-person pod. I ran the cadence.

  • Cadence: 2-week sprints; I ran daily standups, refinement, and reviews.
  • With the PM function: roadmap flowed down; evidence flowed back up. The scope call later in this story is that loop working.
  • With design: refinement loop. We A/B-tested UI variants and completion CTAs through our Customer Intelligence Dashboard; engineering feasibility fed back before design over-committed.
  • With data science and AI: two rules. Deterministic first: no black box, every insight reproducible with an evidence chain. And LLM insight-card prompt testing behind format guardrails.

From the MVP spec · exit criteria I held it to
  • Ranking reproducible given the same inputs
  • Every ranked PO carries a machine-readable explanation
  • Snapshot deltas identify new top-10 entrants, rank jumps, risk-bucket changes
  • One plant runs the daily refresh end-to-end in under 10 minutes

Acceptance criteria for an AI-adjacent feature are not "the model is accurate." They are: reproducible, explained, and fast enough for the 6 AM planning meeting.

03 · Execution: the blocker

When the forms stalled.

Data entry forms were stuck between the client's backend data, too many stakeholders, and a team not yet using AI tooling well. I sequenced the work and standardized the workflow.

2 · SCHEMAdeliberated with and owned by backend

3 · API CONTRACTmock payloads so frontend builds in parallel
→
4 · FULL-STACK BUILDswap mocks for live endpoints
→
5 · REVIEWfinal design + client SME sign-off
  • Parallel lanes: multiple forms ran through this pipeline simultaneously; status checked every day in standup.
  • Result: the stall broke; the workflow became the team standard (PRD → TRD, schema-first).

Data uncertainty as a product rule

POs with no derivable promise date are marked Data Incomplete and visibly excluded from ranking. "% of ranked POs using assumptions" is an instrumented KPI. The mess is made visible and priced, never hidden inside a model.

03 · Execution: scope decisions

Prioritise. And buy.

Call 1 · MVP over factory intelligence

The situation
The MVP and factory-intelligence enhancements were running simultaneously, competing for the same pod.
The call
I prioritised the MVP: the product with the committed partner and the clearest path to impact.
The result
The MVP shipped on plan; factory intelligence continued behind it and stood on its own later.

Call 2 · Buy the knowledge graph

The situation
The traceability core needed a knowledge graph and querying engine: deep, specialist work.
The call
We deliberately did not build it. We partnered with a specialist (NeuralAxis) with deliverables contracted in the roadmap: batch and route representation, 95% mass balance, cycle times, cost per batch.
The result
Our pod stayed focused where our differentiation was: forms, dashboards, reports, and the planning logic on top of the graph.

05 · Lessons learned

Two lessons and one honest do-over.

LESSON 1

Explainability buys adoption

Planners trusted the top-2-reasons column before they trusted the score. Deterministic, evidence-chain insights became a product principle, not a nice-to-have.

LESSON 2

Data quality is a product surface

Make the mess visible and priced: Data Incomplete flags, mass-balance breaks, instrumented assumptions. Never let a model hide it.

WHAT I WOULD CHANGE

Standardize earlier

Institute the schema-first PRD → TRD workflow from sprint 0 instead of creating it mid-way when the forms stalled, and bring client SMEs into refinement from day one rather than at design review.
