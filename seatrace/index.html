02 · Team structure & collaboration

A 14-person pod. I ran the cadence.

  • Cadence: 2-week sprints; I ran daily standups, refinement, and reviews.
  • With the PM function: roadmap flowed down; evidence flowed back up. The scope call later in this story is that loop working.

03 · Execution & Product Owner (PO) Activities

Managing Sprint Cadence, Blockers & Capacity

Sprint Cadence & Rituals

  • Sprint Length: Managed 11-day sprints, using daily planning calls and core team alignment meetings.
  • Touchpoints: Daily stand-ups twice a day (10:00 AM and 9:00 PM), and regular client/SME feedback calls to ensure alignment.

Unblocking and Capacity Management

  • Engineering Blockers: Unblocked the technical team expeditiously by connecting engineers directly with subject matter experts (SMEs) to bypass domain translation delays.
  • Capacity Adjustments: Handled scope adjustments (increase or decrease) dynamically by hiring additional part-time members to meet timeline pressures.

Data Uncertainty as a Product Rule

Purchase Orders (POs) with no derivable promise date are marked Data Incomplete and visibly excluded from the ranking queue.

"% of ranked POs using assumptions" is an instrumented product KPI. The data mess is exposed and priced, never hidden inside a model's prediction.

03 · Execution & Product Owner (PO) Activities

AI/ML: Handling Uncertainty, Spikes & Data Issues

Client Insights & LLM Guardrails

  • Insights at Scale: Designed and deployed LLM prompts in a parallel pod to generate client-facing insights at scale.
  • Evaluation and Evals: Utilized an LLM-as-a-judge system, structured evaluation sets, and competing LLMs to verify that guardrails were respected and formatting/semantic criteria met.

Precision-Recall and Data Flow

  • Strategic Trade-offs: The Precision-Recall trade-off was decided and locked at the very beginning of the product planning stage, rather than left as a post-implementation tuning task.
  • Cross-Collaboration: Handled data flow and schema anomalies through close cross-collaboration between the backend and full-stack engineering teams to resolve pipeline blockages.

The Precision-Recall Rule

In automated batch verification, false positives (wrongly flagging a batch) delay shipping, while false negatives (missing a break) cause expensive recalls.

Establishing operational alignment early on allowed the engineering team to optimize parameters specifically for the business cost curve.

05 · Lessons learned

Two lessons and one honest do-over.

LESSON 1

Explainability buys adoption

Planners trusted the top-2-reasons column before they trusted the score. Deterministic, evidence-chain insights became a product principle, not a nice-to-have.

LESSON 2

Data quality is a product surface

Make the mess visible and priced: Data Incomplete flags, mass-balance breaks, instrumented assumptions. Never let a model hide it.

WHAT I WOULD CHANGE

Standardize earlier

Institute the schema-first PRD → TRD workflow from sprint 0 instead of creating it mid-way when the forms stalled, and bring client SMEs into refinement from day one rather than at design review.
